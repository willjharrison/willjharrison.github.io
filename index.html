Sensory Intelligence Research Group

From neurons to behaviour through computation and experiment.

About

The Sensory Intelligence Research Group is interested in understanding how the brain gives rise to our thoughts, feelings, and actions. Led by Dr Will Harrison at the University of the Sunshine Coast, our mission is to work across levels of explanation — empirical, theoretical, and philosophical — to uncover how the brain turns raw sensory signals into meaningful perception and action.

Pushing Back On A.I.

Modern artificial intelligence has achieved remarkable feats, but we suggest it is not yet fit for purpose for understanding many topics in neuroscience and cognition. We cannot explain the brain by simply comparing it to another system that is itself opaque and requires entire fields to interpret.

Rather than substituting one black box for another, we ground our explanations in biological data and theoretical principles. We study what neurons compute, how populations encode information, and how these processes support human experience.

Research Themes

Robust Psychophysics

Designing controlled behavioural experiments to precisely measure perceptual thresholds, biases, and decision rules.

Active Exploration

Using high-resolution eye tracking to study how gaze patterns and eye movements reveal strategies for extracting information.

Working Memory

Developing models to explain storage limits, fidelity, and the trade-offs in how visual information is maintained over time.

Peripheral Vision

Investigating crowding, pooling, and lossy compression principles that shape perception outside the centre of gaze.

Meta-Cognition

Examining how confidence judgements are formed and how people monitor and control their own cognitive processes.

Computational Modelling

Building Bayesian observer models and efficient coding frameworks to bridge behaviour, neural coding, and environment statistics.
